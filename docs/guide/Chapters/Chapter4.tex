% Chapter 4: conserved moments and the discrete equilibrium

\chapter{Moments and equilibrium}

\label{Chapter4}

\file{fluxlb/core/equilibrium.py} holds the two halves of the map between populations and
macroscopic fields: \code{moments()} projects a population field onto the collision
invariants, and \code{equilibrium()} reconstructs the discrete Maxwell--Boltzmann
distribution that carries them, as a Hermite expansion truncated at the order the lattice's
quadrature supports. \Cref{sec:eq-theory} gives the theory and \cref{sec:eq-impl} the
implementation notes.

%----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\section{Theory}\label{sec:eq-theory}

The density and momentum are the zeroth and first velocity moments of the populations,
\begin{equation}
\rho = \sum_i f_i, \qquad \rho\vu = \sum_i \vc_i f_i,
\label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
and the velocity is their ratio. For every collision operator in the classical core (BGK,
TRT, MRT) these two moments are collision invariants: the post-collision populations have
the same $\rho$ and $\rho\vu$ as the pre-collision ones. Since the equilibrium is built from
these values, $f$ and $f^{\mathrm{eq}}$ have the same $\rho$ and $\rho\vu$ by construction,
and mass and momentum conservation of the solver reduce to \code{moments()} returning the
same values before and after collision. Rounding error in $\rho$ and $\vu$ enters
$f^{\mathrm{eq}}$ at the next step and is not corrected by the relaxation, which is the
reason for the accumulation policy of \cref{sec:precision}.

With the Gauss--Hermite construction of \cref{sec:lattice-theory}, the continuous
Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution at the lattice reference temperature is expanded in tensor
Hermite polynomials of the scaled velocity $\boldsymbol{\xi} = \vc/\cs$ and evaluated at the
discrete velocities, with the lattice weights as the quadrature weights. Truncating at order
$N$ gives
\begin{equation}
f^{\mathrm{eq}}_i = w_i\,\rho \sum_{n=0}^{N} \frac{1}{n!}\,
\mathbf{a}^{(n)} : \mathbf{H}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_i),
\qquad \mathbf{a}^{(n)} = \left(\frac{\vu}{\cs}\right)^{\otimes n},
\label{eq:hermite-eq}
\end{equation}
where the isothermal Hermite coefficients $\mathbf{a}^{(n)}$ are the $n$-fold tensor products
of the scaled velocity \parencite{Grad1949,ShanYuanChen2006}. Contracting each Hermite
tensor with $\vu$ repeated $n$ times collapses the terms to polynomials in
$\vc_i\cdot\vu$ and $\vu\cdot\vu$ alone. Writing $\cs^2$ for the reference temperature in
lattice units, the series is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f^{\mathrm{eq}}_i = w_i\,\rho\,\Big[\,1
&+ \frac{\vc_i\cdot\vu}{\cs^2}
 + \frac{(\vc_i\cdot\vu)^2 - \cs^2\,\vu\cdot\vu}{2\cs^4} \\
&+ \frac{(\vc_i\cdot\vu)^3 - 3\cs^2\,(\vc_i\cdot\vu)\,\vu\cdot\vu}{6\cs^6} \\
&+ \frac{(\vc_i\cdot\vu)^4 - 6\cs^2\,(\vc_i\cdot\vu)^2\,\vu\cdot\vu
        + 3\cs^4\,(\vu\cdot\vu)^2}{24\cs^8}
 + \cdots\Big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:hermite-terms}
\end{equation}
truncated after the term of order $N$ in $\vu$. Order $N$ includes every term below it.
The first three terms are the second-order equilibrium of
\textcite{QianHumieresLallemand1992}.

Because the Hermite polynomials are orthogonal under the Gaussian weight, a truncation at
order $N$ reproduces the velocity moments of the Maxwell--Boltzmann distribution exactly up
to order $N$, provided the quadrature integrates polynomials of degree $2N$ exactly
\parencite{ShanYuanChen2006}. The algebraic degree of the lattice is its isotropy order
(\cref{sec:lattice-theory}), so the highest admissible truncation is
$N_{\max} = \lfloor \code{isotropy\_order}/2 \rfloor$. \code{Lattice.equilibrium\_order}
stores this value and the constructor enforces $2N_{\max} \le \code{isotropy\_order}$ for
every subclass. A higher truncation on a lattice whose quadrature cannot represent it adds
terms with incorrect moments, so requesting one raises \code{ValueError}.

The admissible orders and the equations they recover:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Order 1} (D2Q5). The linear equilibrium for scalar transport. The zeroth and
      first moments are exact, and the advection--diffusion equation requires no others.
      \Cref{Chapter3} gives the reason order~2 is not offered on this lattice.
\item \textbf{Order 2} (D2Q9, D3Q19, D3Q27). The isothermal Navier--Stokes equilibrium. The
      second moment is exactly $\Pi^{\mathrm{eq}}_{\alpha\beta} = \rho\cs^2\delta_{\alpha\beta}
      + \rho u_\alpha u_\beta$, as required by the Chapman--Enskog expansion for the
      Newtonian stress. The third moment is
      $\rho\cs^2(u_\alpha\delta_{\beta\gamma} + \text{perms})$ without the
      $\rho u_\alpha u_\beta u_\gamma$ part; this is the $O(u^3)$ error of the standard
      lattices.
\item \textbf{Orders 3 and 4} (D2Q37). The third and fourth moments are exact. The third
      includes the $\rho u_\alpha u_\beta u_\gamma$ term, which removes the cubic error and,
      with a temperature field, gives the heat flux; the fourth is required for the energy
      equation \parencite{Philippi2006,Sbragaglia2009}. The implementation is isothermal:
      the coefficients in \cref{eq:hermite-terms} are those at the reference temperature,
      and the terms in the temperature deviation required by a thermal model are not
      implemented.
\end{itemize}
The truncation error in the velocity is of order $\mathrm{Ma}^{N+1}$ at every order, so a
low Mach number is assumed.

\section{Implementation}\label{sec:eq-impl}

\paragraph{Shapes.}
Populations are directions-first, $f[Q, \ast]$, and velocities components-first,
$\vu[D, \ast]$, where $\ast$ stands for the spatial axes. Every reduction is over
\code{dim=0}, and \code{lattice.c} with shape $[Q, D]$ contracts against either field
directly. The two contractions are
\begin{lstlisting}[style=py]
rho = torch.sum(f, dim=0)                           # [*]
mom = torch.einsum("qa,q...->a...", lattice.c, f)   # [D, *]
cu  = torch.einsum("qa,a...->q...", lattice.c, u)   # [Q, *]
\end{lstlisting}
The ellipsis absorbs the spatial axes; the labelled axis is always the leading one. The
weights broadcast against $[Q, \ast]$ through
\code{lattice.w.reshape(-1, *([1] * lattice.D))}.

\paragraph{\code{moments(lattice, f)}.}
Returns \code{(rho, u)} with shapes $[\ast]$ and $[D, \ast]$ in the dtype of \code{f}.
Internally \code{f} and \code{lattice.c} are cast to \code{accumulation\_dtype(f.device)},
both reductions and the division $\vu = \rho\vu/\rho$ are performed there, and the results
are cast back. This is the single place the fp32-compute / fp64-accumulation policy of
\cref{sec:precision} is applied; collision operators and diagnostics call it rather than
reducing \code{f} themselves. Note that the momentum contraction has no reduction
\code{dtype} argument, so unlike the \code{sum} idiom in \cref{sec:precision} an fp64 copy
of \code{f} is materialised for the duration of the call. The cast is differentiable, so
gradients flow through \code{moments()} to \code{f}.

\paragraph{\code{equilibrium(lattice, rho, u, order=None)}.}
Returns $f^{\mathrm{eq}}$ with shape $[Q, \ast]$ in the dtype of \code{rho}. \code{order}
defaults to \code{lattice.equilibrium\_order} and must satisfy
$1 \le \code{order} \le \code{lattice.equilibrium\_order}$; anything else raises
\code{ValueError} naming the lattice and its range. A lower order than the lattice supports
is allowed, so the standard order-2 form can be compared against order~4 on D2Q37. The body
binds $\vc_i\cdot\vu$ and $\vu\cdot\vu$ once and accumulates the terms of
\cref{eq:hermite-terms} in a cumulative chain of \code{if order >= n} branches, returning
\code{w * rho * series} as a single broadcast expression:
\begin{lstlisting}[style=py]
w   = lattice.w.reshape(-1, *([1] * lattice.D))
cs2 = lattice.cs2
cu  = torch.einsum("qa,a...->q...", lattice.c, u)
uu  = torch.sum(u * u, dim=0)
series = 1.0 + cu / cs2
if order >= 2:
    series = series + (cu**2 - cs2 * uu) / (2 * cs2**2)
if order >= 3:
    series = series + ...                           # third-order term
if order >= 4:
    series = series + ...                           # fourth-order term
return w * rho * series
\end{lstlisting}
There is no accumulation cast. The equilibrium is a pointwise reconstruction, not a
reduction over directions, so its rounding error does not enter the invariants and the
compute dtype is used. There are no in-place operations, so gradients flow to \code{rho},
\code{u}, and through \code{lattice.cs2} if it is made learnable.

\paragraph{\code{Lattice.equilibrium\_order}.}
A class attribute beside \code{isotropy\_order}, set to
$\lfloor\code{isotropy\_order}/2\rfloor$ on every implemented lattice
(\cref{tab:catalogue}). \code{Lattice.\_\_init\_\_} raises \code{ValueError} if a subclass
declares an order its isotropy cannot support, so a mismatched lattice cannot be
constructed.

\paragraph{Usage.}
\begin{lstlisting}[style=py]
from fluxlb.core.equilibrium import equilibrium, moments
from fluxlb.core.lattices import D2Q9, D2Q37

lat = D2Q9()
rho, u = moments(lat, f)                 # fp64 accumulation, fp32 results
feq = equilibrium(lat, rho, u)           # order 2, the lattice default

lat37 = D2Q37()
feq4 = equilibrium(lat37, rho, u)        # order 4
feq2 = equilibrium(lat37, rho, u, order=2)   # explicit lower order
equilibrium(lat, rho, u, order=3)        # ValueError: outside 1..2 for D2Q9
\end{lstlisting}
